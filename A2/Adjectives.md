# A2 Grammar: Adjectives (Adjektive)

At the A2 level, we introduce adjective declension (adding endings to adjectives that sit directly in front of nouns) and adjective comparison.

---

## 1. Adjective Declension: After Definite Articles (Weak Declension)

When an adjective is placed between a definite article (*der, die, das*) and a noun, it must take a declension ending. At A2, we focus on the most common declension pattern (Weak Declension).

The endings are very straightforward: either `-e` or `-en`.

| Case | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Nominative** | -e | -e | -e | -en |
| **Accusative** | -en | -e | -e | -en |
| **Dative** | -en | -en | -en | -en |

### Key Examples in Context:
- **Nominative**: 
  - Der klein**e** Hund schläft. (The small dog sleeps.)
  - Die schön**e** Frau singt. (The beautiful woman sings.)
- **Accusative**: 
  - Ich kaufe den rot**en** Apfel (masculine accusative -> `-en`).
  - Ich mag das neu**es** Auto. (The neuter accusative -> `-e`).
- **Dative**:
  - Ich helfe dem alt**en** Mann (masculine dative -> `-en`).
  - Ich spiele mit der nett**en** Frau (feminine dative -> `-en`).

---

## 2. Adjective Comparison (Steigerung)

To compare things, adjectives have three forms: Positive, Comparative (*Komparativ*), and Superlative (*Superlativ*).

### A. Regular Form:
- Add `-er` for the **Comparative**.
- Use **am** + adjective ending in **-sten** for the **Superlative**.

| Positive | Comparative (+ *als*) | Superlative |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **schnell** (fast) | schneller (faster) | am schnellsten (fastest) |
| **klein** (small) | kleiner (smaller) | am kleinsten (smallest) |
| **billig** (cheap) | billiger (cheaper) | am billigsten (cheapest) |

- **Example**: Mein Auto ist **schneller als** dein Fahrrad. (My car is faster than your bicycle.)
- **Example**: Das Flugzeug ist **am schnellsten**. (The airplane is the fastest.)

---

### B. Umlaut Shift:
One-syllable adjectives with `a`, `o`, or `u` usually receive an umlaut in their comparative and superlative forms:

| Positive | Comparative | Superlative |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **alt** (old) | **ä**lter | am **ä**ltesten |
| **groß** (big/tall) | gr**ö**ßer | am gr**ö**ßten |
| **warm** (warm) | w**ä**rmer | am w**ä**rmesten |
| **jung** (young) | j**ü**nger | am j**ü**ngsten |

---

### C. Irregular Comparison:
These three highly common forms are irregular and must be memorized:

| Positive | Comparative | Superlative |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **gut** (good) | besser (better) | am besten (best) |
| **viel** (much / a lot) | mehr (more) | am meisten (most) |
| **gern** (gladly / like to) | lieber (prefer to) | am liebsten (favorite / most gladly) |

- **Example**: Ich trinke Wasser, aber ich trinke **lieber** Kaffee. Am **liebsten** trinke ich Tee. (I drink water, but I prefer to drink coffee. Most of all, I like to drink tea.)
