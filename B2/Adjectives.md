# B2 Grammar: Adjectives (Adjektive)

At the B2 level, you refine your understanding of adjective usage, examining declensions in complex conjunction structures and dealing with nouns functioning as adjectives.

---

## 1. Adjective Declension after Two-Part Conjunctions
When describing items connected by two-part conjunctions (like *sowohl...als auch* or *weder...noch*), the adjectives follow regular declension rules but require close attention to whether the article carries over.

- **sowohl ... als auch** (both ... as well as):
  - Ich trinke sowohl **heißen** Kaffee als auch **kalten** Tee. (Strong declension: Masculine Accusative `-en` for both).
- **weder ... noch** (neither ... nor):
  - Er hat weder **ein neues** Auto noch **ein großes** Haus. (Mixed declension: Neuter/Masc Accusative endings).

---

## 2. Nouns Functioning as Adjectives (Adjektivische Nomen)
German has words that are grammatically adjectives (i.e. they decline exactly like adjectives) but function as nouns. They are capitalized.

### Common Examples:
- **der Angestellte** (the employee - masculine) / **die Angestellte** (the employee - feminine)
- **der Verwandte** (the relative) / **die Verwandte** (the relative)
- **der Jugendliche** (the youth/teenager)
- **der Beamte** (the civil servant)

### Declension Rules:
Since they are adjectives at heart, they follow the three declension tables (Weak, Mixed, or Strong) depending on the article.

#### 1. After Definite Article (Weak Declension):
- *der Angestellte* (Nom. Masc. -> `-e`)
- *ich sehe den Angestellt**en*** (Acc. Masc. -> `-en`)
- *ich helfe dem Angestellt**en*** (Dat. Masc. -> `-en`)

#### 2. After Indefinite Article (Mixed Declension):
- *ein Angestellt**er*** (Nom. Masc. -> `-er`)
- *ich sehe einen Angestellt**en*** (Acc. Masc. -> `-en`)

#### 3. Without Preceding Article (Strong Declension):
- *Angestellt**e*** (Nom. Plur. -> `-e`): **Angestellte** streiken. (Employees are striking.)
- *mit Angestellt**en*** (Dat. Plur. -> `-en`): Er spricht mit **Angestellten**.
