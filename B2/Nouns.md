# B2 Grammar: Nouns (Nomen) - Nominalization (Nominalstil)

At the B2 level, your German becomes more formal, academic, and professional. One of the main features of formal written German is the **Nominal Style** (*Nominalstil*), which turns actions (verbs) or states (adjectives) into nouns.

---

## 1. What is Nominalization?
Nominalization is the process of converting verbs, adjectives, or prepositions into nouns. This is heavily used in news, reports, business communication, and scientific texts because it condenses information.

- **Verbal Style (Casual/Spoken)**: *Wenn man Auto fährt*, muss man sich konzentrieren. (When driving a car, one must concentrate.)
- **Nominal Style (Formal/Written)**: *Beim Autofahren* ist Konzentration erforderlich. (During car-driving, concentration is required.)

---

## 2. Converting Verbs to Nouns

There are two primary ways to turn verbs into nouns:

### A. Direct Infinitives (Neuter)
Take the infinitive of the verb, capitalize it, and use it with the article **das**.
- *essen* (to eat) -> **das Essen** (the food/eating)
- *lernen* (to learn) -> **das Lernen** (learning)
- **Example**: **Das Schreiben** von Berichten dauert lange. (The writing of reports takes a long time.)

### B. Verb Stems with Suffixes
Many verbs can be transformed by adding specific suffixes (which determine the gender of the noun):

- **`-ung`** (always Feminine):
  - *befreien* (to free) -> **die Befreiung** (liberation)
  - *entscheiden* (to decide) -> **die Entscheidung** (decision)
- **`-tion`** / **`-ion`** (always Feminine):
  - *diskutieren* (to discuss) -> **die Diskussion** (discussion)
  - *produzieren* (to produce) -> **die Produktion** (production)
- **`-er`** (agent, Masculine):
  - *leiten* (to lead) -> **der Leiter** (leader)

---

## 3. Converting Adjectives to Nouns
Adjectives can also be nominalized. They are capitalized and usually take neuter or masculine/feminine endings depending on who or what they refer to. Nominalized adjectives still follow regular adjective declension rules.

### A. Referring to Abstract Concepts (Neuter)
Usually preceded by *etwas*, *nichts*, *viel*, or *wenig*.
- *neu* (new) -> etwas **Neues** (something new)
- *gut* (good) -> nichts **Gutes** (nothing good)
- *interessant* (interesting) -> viel **Interessantes** (much interesting stuff)

### B. Referring to People (Masculine / Feminine)
- *krank* (sick) -> **der Kranke** (the sick man) / **die Kranke** (the sick woman)
- *deutsch* (German) -> **der Deutsche** (the German man) / **die Deutsche** (the German woman)
