# C1 Grammar: Adjectives (Adjektive) - Extended Attribute Structures

At the C1 level, you will encounter highly complex sentence structures in academic writing, quality newspapers, and literature. One of these structures is the **Extended Attribute** (*erweitertes Attribut*), where a relative clause is condensed and embedded directly between the article and the noun as an adjectival phrase.

---

## 1. What is an Extended Attribute?

In English, we describe a noun with a relative clause placed *after* the noun. German can do this too (using *Relativsätze*), but it also has the option to pull all that information *before* the noun, wrapping it inside the adjective declension.

- **Relative Clause (Standard)**: 
  - Die Konferenz, *die von vielen Wissenschaftlern besucht wurde*, war ein Erfolg. (The conference, which was attended by many scientists, was a success.)
- **Extended Attribute (C1 / Academic)**: 
  - Die **von vielen Wissenschaftlern besuchte** Konferenz war ein Erfolg. (Literally: The *by-many-scientists-attended* conference was a success.)

---

## 2. Structure and Reading Strategy

An extended attribute is structured like a sandwich:

$$\text{[Article]} + \text{\{\{Extended Information\}\}} + \text{[Adjective]} + \text{[Noun]}$$

### How to analyze it:
1. Identify the **article** (e.g., *die, der, ein*) and look forward to find the matching **noun** that shares the same gender/case ending.
2. The core action is described by the **adjective/participle** (e.g., *besuchte*, *stattfindende*, *gelesene*) sitting directly before the noun.
3. Everything in between the article and the adjective is the **modifying information** (prepositional phrases, adverbs, objects).

---

## 3. Practical Examples

### Example A: Passive Past Participle
- *Relative Clause*: Der Bericht, *der gestern im Parlament diskutiert wurde*, ist vertraulich.
- *Extended Attribute*: Der **gestern im Parlament diskutierte** Bericht ist vertraulich. (The report discussed in parliament yesterday is confidential.)

### Example B: Active Present Participle (Partizip I)
- *Relative Clause*: Die Kinder, *die auf der Straße laut spielen*, stören die Nachbarn.
- *Extended Attribute*: Die **auf der Straße laut spielenden** Kinder stören die Nachbarn. (The children playing loudly on the street disturb the neighbors.)

### Example C: Gerundive (*zu* + Present Participle)
Conveys necessity or feasibility (must be done / can be done).
- *Relative Clause*: Die Aufgaben, *die schnell gelöst werden müssen*, sind schwer.
- *Extended Attribute*: Die **schnell zu lösenden** Aufgaben sind schwer. (The tasks to be solved quickly are difficult.)
